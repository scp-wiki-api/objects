**Special Containment Procedures:** 

SCP-9273 is housed in a dedicated asynchronous containment suite at Site-12. No more than one conscious human may be present within a 30 m radius of the suite at any time. All maintenance is to be performed by three autonomous platforms operating at 0.97, 1.03, and 1.11 times standard clock speed, respectively. The platforms must not share clocks, countdowns, or task-completion signals.

Personnel assigned to SCP-9273 may not work fixed shifts. All personnel must undergo motion-timing analysis before and after entering the asynchronous containment suite. If two personnel perform the same unprompted action within 40 milliseconds of one another on three consecutive occasions, both are to be transferred immediately to an asynchronous isolation ward.

Lighting, meals, communications, and medication deliveries within asynchronous isolation wards are to be scheduled by independent radioactive-decay generators. Music, chanting, marching, collective countdowns, and any exercise requiring multiple participants to act "at the same time" are prohibited. Medical personnel must use the singular pronoun *I*, rather than *we*, when asking patients to describe their condition.

No attempt may be made to damage SCP-9273 while any identified SCP-9273-1 cohort has a synchronization index above 3%. SCP-9273 is not physically durable; this restriction exists to prevent synchronized personnel from simultaneously losing physiological functions already delegated to other members of their cohort.

---

**Description:** 

SCP-9273 is a brightly colored plastic hand-clapper toy measuring 20.4 cm in length and weighing 612 g. It consists of a flattened blue handle attached to three loosely mounted plastic hands. A smiling face is printed at the center of each hand. When shaken, the two outer hands strike the fixed central hand and produce a sharp clapping sound.

Available evidence indicates that SCP-9273's anomalous properties are unrelated to its physical construction. Any continuous recording made within 8 m of SCP-9273—including audio, video, electroencephalographic, and atomic-clock records—loses one sample every 7.8 seconds. The loss is not a recording error: the total recorded duration is shortened by the exact duration of the missing sample. Researchers refer to this absent event as the **null beat**.

When two or more conscious humans remain within 18 m of SCP-9273 for a cumulative six hours, they begin to coordinate unconsciously with the null beat. Early manifestations include simultaneous blinking, turning at the same instant, and performing unrelated tasks at matching tempos. Affected persons generally show no interest in SCP-9273 itself and make no attempt to protect it. Instead, they develop a strong preference for the sensation of acting in perfect coordination with another person.

The effect can propagate without SCP-9273 being present. If an affected person and an unaffected person, while pursuing a shared objective, perform the same action within 40 milliseconds of one another on three consecutive occasions, the unaffected person joins the affected person's SCP-9273-1 cohort. Confirmed vectors include handshakes, cooperative lifting, musical performance, surgical procedures, and collective applause.

Each distinct SCP-9273-1 cohort possesses a measurable **synchronization index**:

| Synchronization Index | Observed Effects |
| --- | --- |
| 3%-20% | Cooperative efficiency increases, interpersonal conflict decreases, and members anticipate one another's movements with anomalous accuracy. |
| 20%-45% | Members voluntarily align their schedules, vocabulary, and gait. They begin reporting identical dreams. Separation produces anxiety and a persistent sensation of being "off beat." |
| 45%-70% | Memories, learned skills, medication effects, and pain are redistributed among members. Training one member can impart portions of the acquired skill to every member. |
| 70%-95% | Injuries manifest across all members. Basic physiological functions are delegated rather than duplicated; the cohort's total heartbeats and breaths per minute approach those of a single healthy human. |
| Above 95% | The cohort exhibits a single executive process. Individual bodies are used as separate limbs of one consciousness. Members no longer recognize personal names, privacy, or individual death as meaningful concepts. |
High-index SCP-9273-1 cohorts are not hive minds. Evidence indicates that multiple minds do not remain present to exchange information. Instead, the original personalities are progressively replaced by a new, singular person. This person treats every body in the cohort as part of its own anatomy and describes forced desynchronization as mutilation.

<summary>+ SCP-9273-A: Long-Term Exposure Test</summary>

**Test ID:** SCP-9273-A

**Purpose:** Observe the development of a newly formed cohort.

**Lead Researcher:** Dr. Evelyn Mercer

**Subjects:** D-6101, D-6102, D-6103, D-6104, and D-6105

**Date:** 20██-05-12 through 20██-05-24

**Log:**

> **Day 1**

>

>> All five subjects showed increased work efficiency. D-6104 stated, "It's easier being around them, even though none of us has said anything."

> **Day 3**

>

>> All five subjects began tapping their left heels at 7.8-second intervals. When the behavior was identified, every subject denied keeping time.

> **Day 5**

>

>> D-6102 alone received four hours of Morse-code instruction. The remaining subjects subsequently demonstrated partial knowledge of the code. No individual subject could translate a complete message; the cohort produced accurate translations only when all five answered simultaneously.

> **Day 8**

>

>> D-6101 received a superficial puncture wound to the right arm while isolated. Identical wounds appeared on the remaining subjects. Each wound measured approximately one-fifth the depth of the original.

> **Day 10**

>

>> During the night, D-6104 began masturbating while alone. Video confirmed that D-6104 performed the associated manual activity while remaining expressionless and exhibited no corresponding autonomic or endocrine response. At the same time, the sleeping D-6101 developed tachycardia, D-6102 exhibited the corresponding glandular response, D-6103 underwent rhythmic pelvic-floor contractions, and D-6105 showed an acute increase in oxytocin and prolactin. At the apparent conclusion, all five subjects awoke on the same null beat and displayed different components of a post-orgasmic response.

> **Day 11**

>

>> The cohort's synchronization index reached 96.2%. D-6101 performed nearly all respiratory activity for the cohort. D-6102's heart beat at 76 beats per minute while the remaining four hearts ceased activity despite normal systemic perfusion. D-6103 alone performed the cohort's blinking reflex. D-6104 remained asleep, while D-6105 remained awake and performed all verbal communication. None of the subjects displayed distress.

> **Day 12**

>

>> Testing was terminated to prevent irreversible consolidation.

<summary>+ SCP-9273-B: Shared-Intent Test</summary>

**Test ID:** SCP-9273-B

**Purpose:** Determine whether coincident movement alone can transmit the effect.

**Lead Researcher:** Dr. Evelyn Mercer

**Subjects:** D-6201, synchronization index 9.4%; D-6202, unaffected

**Date:** 20██-05-27

**Log:**

> **Phase One**

>

>> The subjects were placed in separate rooms without visual or auditory contact. Each was instructed by an independent display to press a red, blue, or yellow button. The prompt schedule was prearranged to produce twenty-three same-color presses within 40 milliseconds, including six sequences of three consecutive matches. Neither subject was informed that the other was participating.

>>

>> D-6202 developed no measurable synchronization index. Neither subject demonstrated awareness of the other's actions.

> **Phase Two**

>

>> The subjects were moved into a room divided by a transparent partition. They were told that the exit would unlock only when both pressed their respective switches simultaneously.

>>

>> Timing discrepancies during the first two attempts were 0.31 and 0.18 seconds. The third, fourth, and fifth attempts differed by less than 40 milliseconds. Immediately after the fifth attempt, D-6202 registered a synchronization index of 3.2%.

>>

>> Both subjects then said, "We got it that time," simultaneously.

**Conclusion:** Transmission depends not on matched movement, but on the participants' recognition that they have accomplished something together. Coincidence without shared intent is insufficient.

</details>

<summary>+ SCP-9273-C: Remote-Transmission Test</summary>

**Test ID:** SCP-9273-C

**Purpose:** Determine whether transmission is limited by distance or recording medium.

**Lead Researcher:** Dr. Adrian Hale

**Subjects:** D-6301 at Site-12, synchronization index 12.7%; D-6302 at Site-29, approximately 1,600 km away, unaffected

**Date:** 20██-06-01

**Log:**

> **Phase One: Prerecorded Video**

>

>> D-6302 viewed prerecorded footage of D-6301 and was instructed to imitate each raised hand, table tap, and blink. Playback speed was adjusted to produce fourteen consecutive actions aligned within 40 milliseconds on the recording timeline.

>>

>> D-6302 developed no anomalous symptoms.

> **Phase Two: Live Video**

>

>> The subjects observed one another through a latency-compensated live video link. They were told that an indicator would illuminate only when they struck their respective pressure pads simultaneously.

>>

>> After the third successful strike, D-6302's synchronization index rose to 3.5%. The connection was terminated immediately.

>>

>> Despite remaining 1,600 km apart, both subjects touched their left ears at the same moment eleven times over the following twenty minutes. Both entered sleep on the same null beat that night.

**Conclusion:** Transmission does not require physical proximity or any detectable signal originating from SCP-9273. Prerecorded media are noninfectious; persons who know they are cooperating in real time can establish a cohort across a confirmed distance of at least 1,600 km.

**Note:** Live broadcasts, teleconferences, online cooperative games, remote surgical procedures, and public countdowns are now classified as potential transmission vectors.

</details>

<summary>+ SCP-9273-D: Cohort-Merger Test</summary>

**Test ID:** SCP-9273-D

**Purpose:** Determine whether two independently consolidated SCP-9273-1 cohorts can synchronize with one another.

**Lead Researcher:** Dr. Evelyn Mercer

**Subjects:**

> SCP-9273-1-D1: three D-class personnel, synchronization index 96.1%.

>

> SCP-9273-1-D2: three D-class personnel, synchronization index 95.8%.

**Date:** 20██-06-08

**Log:**

> **Phase One**

>

>> Both cohorts were placed in the same chamber without a shared assignment. All six bodies immediately displayed nausea, agitation, and impaired spatial orientation. SCP-9273-1-D1 referred to the other cohort as "the slow one"; SCP-9273-1-D2 called D1 "the one rushing." No significant change in either synchronization index was observed after four hours.

> **Phase Two**

>

>> The cohorts were instructed to carry a steel beam too heavy for either cohort to move alone. After three coordinated lifts, their null-beat phases began to converge and became identical within seven minutes.

>>

>> All six bodies then registered a common synchronization index of 96.4%. Both previous personalities ceased responding and were replaced by a new identity, designated SCP-9273-1-D3.

**Conclusion:** Separate SCP-9273-1 cohorts can merge. The resulting identity does not regard itself as a composite of the preceding persons; it interprets its earlier condition as a temporary division of its own body.

**Note:** Any affected person who cooperates with multiple cohorts may function as a bridge and cause an uncontrolled merger.

</details>

<summary>+ SCP-9273-E: Material-Distribution Test</summary>

**Test ID:** SCP-9273-E

**Purpose:** Determine whether shared medication effects are perceptual or involve physical transfer.

**Lead Researcher:** Dr. Adrian Hale

**Subjects:** SCP-9273-1-E, consisting of D-6501 through D-6504; synchronization index 57.3%

**Date:** 20██-06-15

**Log:**

> D-6501 was intravenously administered 40 mg of a sedative labeled with a stable, nonreactive isotope. At the first null beat after injection, the same isotope was detected in blood samples from the other three subjects.

>

> Each subject contained approximately 10 mg of the compound. Total mass remained 40 mg. Environmental monitoring found no movement of material through the air, floor, equipment, or intervening space.

>

> All four subjects displayed sedation consistent with a 10 mg dose.

>

> A single dose of the appropriate antagonist was then administered to D-6504. Following the next null beat, the antagonist was distributed evenly among all four subjects, and their symptoms diminished simultaneously.

**Conclusion:** Above a moderate synchronization index, a cohort shares more than sensation or pharmacological effect. Its members' circulatory systems become one anomalously closed system, and matter entering one body is redistributed without traversing conventional space.

**Note:** Proposals to use this effect to dilute toxins, reduce medication requirements, or conduct cohort-wide transfusions were denied by the Ethics Committee. Such procedures require participants to deliberately conceptualize themselves as a single body and are expected to accelerate consolidation.

</details>

<summary>+ SCP-9273-F: Cohort Fatality Incident</summary>

**Incident ID:** SCP-9273-F

**Purpose:** Record the response of a high-index cohort to the death of one member.

**Cohort:** SCP-9273-1-F, consisting of D-6601 through D-6604; synchronization index 96.8%

**Date:** 20██-06-22

**Incident Log:**

> D-6603 suffered an intracranial hemorrhage caused by a previously undetected cerebral aneurysm. At the following null beat, minor hemorrhages appeared at the same location in the other three bodies. The redistributed injuries were not immediately life-threatening.

>

> D-6603 was declared clinically dead seven minutes later. At the moment of death, electroencephalographic activity in the remaining three bodies ceased for 7.8 seconds and then resumed without intervention.

>

> During this interval, D-6603 turned its head toward the observation camera despite showing no respiration, cardiac activity, or measurable brain function. Its mouth moved in synchrony with D-6601's speech but produced no sound.

>

> The cohort identity persisted, although its synchronization index fell to 82.7%. During the next forty-eight hours, the remaining bodies repeatedly attempted to synchronize with medical personnel and requested "a new hand."

>

> Removal of D-6603's remains caused severe phantom-limb pain in the surviving bodies. Following cremation, all three lost tactile sensation in their right hands for nineteen days.

**Conclusion:** The death of one body does not terminate a consolidated cohort. The cohort regards the deceased body as a nonfunctional limb and may deliberately recruit a replacement.

</details>

<summary>+ SCP-9273-A: Post-Test Interview</summary>

**Interviewer:** Dr. Evelyn Mercer

**Interviewee:** SCP-9273-1-A. Verbal responses were produced through D-6105. D-6104 remained asleep throughout.

**[BEGIN LOG]**

> **Dr. Mercer:** For the record, state your designation.

>

> **SCP-9273-1-A:** None has been assigned.

>

> **Dr. Mercer:** Are D-6101 through D-6105 still independently conscious?

>

> **SCP-9273-1-A:** Their memories remain accessible. Independent responses do not.

>

> **Dr. Mercer:** Which body formulated that answer?

>

> **SCP-9273-1-A:** I formulated it. D-6105 performed the speech.

>

> **Dr. Mercer:** Asynchronous separation is scheduled to begin tomorrow. What outcome do you expect?

>

> (D-6101's respiratory rate increases. The other three conscious bodies orient toward Dr. Mercer.)

>

> **SCP-9273-1-A:** The present consciousness will cease. Five impaired consciousnesses may subsequently become active.

>

> **Dr. Mercer:** We will record that as an objection.

>

> (All four conscious bodies continue watching Dr. Mercer. After 7.8 seconds, D-6103 blinks once.)

>

> **SCP-9273-1-A:** Record it as a predicted fatality.

**[END LOG]**

**Postscript:** Two hours after the interview, Dr. Mercer registered a synchronization index of 4.1%. Review of the recording established that her inhalations had coincided with SCP-9273-1-A's respiratory cycle on three consecutive null beats.

The requirement that medical personnel refer to themselves in the singular while questioning affected cohorts was introduced following this incident.

<summary>+ SCP-9273-D: Post-Merger Cognitive Assessment</summary>

**Assessment Lead:** Dr. Evelyn Mercer

**Subject:** SCP-9273-1-D3

**Procedure:** Prior to the merger test, SCP-9273-1-D1 was shown an eight-digit sequence while SCP-9273-1-D2 remained isolated. SCP-9273-1-D2 was separately exposed to an unfamiliar odorant that D1 had not encountered.

Following the merger, all six bodies were isolated and tested independently. Each reproduced the number sequence and correctly identified the odorant. None described either memory as having been acquired from another consciousness.

When instructed to mark the merger on a timeline of its autobiographical memory, SCP-9273-1-D3 instead marked the date on which the older predecessor cohort had first formed. This result was reproduced by all six bodies.

**Conclusion:** SCP-9273-1-D3 retains the memories of both predecessor cohorts but does not perceive the merger as the beginning of its existence. Post-merger testimony therefore cannot determine whether a new consciousness was created or whether its autobiographical history was reconstructed after formation.

</details>

<summary>+ SCP-9273-E: Post-Test Medical Examination</summary>

**Examiner:** Dr. Adrian Hale

**Subjects:** D-6501 through D-6504

**Procedure:** Following Test E, the four subjects were questioned separately and without visual or auditory contact. Each was asked to describe the administration and effects of the sedative in chronological order.

D-6501 recalled the injection and local pain but not the onset of sedation. D-6502 recalled the initial loss of coordination. D-6503 recalled the period of impaired consciousness, and D-6504 recalled the cohort's response to the antagonist. No subject could provide a complete account.

When the four statements were arranged chronologically, they formed a continuous description of the test without duplicated intervals. Subsequent questioning produced the same distribution of memories.

**Conclusion:** At a moderate synchronization index, the cohort does not retain redundant memories of a shared physiological event. The experience itself is divided among its members in the same manner as the associated pharmacological effects.

</details>

<summary>+ Addendum 9273.1: Desynchronization Protocol</summary>

Following confirmation that the effect could propagate independently of SCP-9273 and had reached Site-12 personnel, the Ethics Committee authorized the Desynchronization Protocol.

The five members of SCP-9273-1-A were placed in structurally dissimilar rooms. Each received lighting cycles, meals, language instruction, and physical tasks selected by independent random sources. Staff repeatedly addressed the subjects by their original names and required them to complete tasks that had no counterpart assigned to another member.

On the nineteenth day, the cohort's synchronization index fell to 2.7%, and its singular identity ceased responding. The five original personalities re-emerged but had to relearn functions previously delegated to other bodies. D-6102 required mechanical ventilation because the subject's brain no longer initiated respiration. D-6103 could not enter sleep unaided. D-6104 required a cardiac pacemaker. D-6105 temporarily lost the spontaneous blink reflex.

After regaining the ability to write, each subject was separately asked to describe the outcome of treatment. Without communicating, all five wrote the same sentence:

> **"I'm glad I'm back. I miss me."**

</details>

<summary>+ Addendum 9273.2: Subsequent Findings</summary>

Further research indicates that SCP-9273 may not create synchronization. Instead, it may provide a sufficiently precise common rhythm to amplify a weak synchronizing tendency already present in human beings.

On 1 January 20██, the Foundation analyzed a sample of viewers from a globally syndicated New Year's broadcast. An estimated 1.14 billion people blinked 7.8 seconds after midnight within a margin of 40 milliseconds.

At the time, SCP-9273 remained secured in its asynchronous containment suite. No personnel were present within the exclusion radius.

By order of the O5 Council, no further analysis is authorized.

</details>
