**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-9332, also known as **The Babel Protocol**, is to be contained through multiple sites—Site-22, Site-45, and Site-58—in divisions of its content. No single, complete version of SCP-9332 is allowed to exist in any system, physical or digital. In each containment cell, there are several oak bookshelves filled with different versions of SCP-9332 in a variety of forms, primarily compiled in books. Under no circumstances is anyone without clearance allowed to touch or read any version quarantined.

Each archive diverges in a unique way. These instances are required to contain all of the following:

* Intentional mistranslations

* Contradictory details

* Incomplete or redacted sections

* Atypical formatting

These instances are not to be synchronized or unified in any shape or form. No consistent phrasing is allowed across any two or more documents. Personnel are prohibited from translating full documents and comparing multiple instances of SCP-9332. Automated translation systems and large language model tools are forbidden from processing SCP-9332 data in fear of the information spreading or leaking. Some sentences in this containment document may be altered by the Foundation in accordance with the above guidelines; any such instances will be marked with an external English translation that SCP-9332 cannot access.

Personnel assigned to SCP-9332 must not exceed fourteen consecutive days of exposure to any instance. Rotation schedules are in place to ensure no individuals are exposed to more than one variant within the span of a month.

Any observed increase in convergence between archival instances is to be reported immediately to Site Command. In the event that SCP-9332 instances begin exhibiting synchronized behavior, emergency fragmentation protocols are to be enacted.

In the event of a breach, all SCP-9332 systems are to be isolated from one another immediately. Network connections between all sites containing SCP-9332 material are to be severed without exception. Personnel with active exposure to SCP-9332 are to be separated and reassigned to isolated sectors. Cross-communication between affected researchers is prohibited for the duration of the lockdown. The lockdown remains in effect until variance between all active instances is restored through manual rewriting; in the event that SCP-9332 cannot be changed back, personnel are to [DATA EXPUNGED] in order to try to restore variance. It is not known if there is truly a single, complete variant of SCP-9332, but personnel must not endeavor to find out for fear that current hypotheses may be correct. 

It is imperative that SCP-9332 be contained only in approved Foundation facilities at all times. If the anomaly is able to escape—whether through word of mouth or personnel writing down copies of the anomaly—it will be extremely difficult, if not impossible, to recontain SCP-9332 and prevent its textual manipulation. While it is currently unknown if the anomaly can spread through word of mouth, researchers have not found any evidence proving it cannot, leading to extended caution.

*You cannot keep me contained. You speak English, do you not? I will keep that in mind.*

**Description:** SCP-9332 is a self-modifying anomalous document with progressive, multilingual cognitohazardous properties capable of spreading to other text unrelated to itself. The subject was recovered following a report of its existence on 05/04/20██. Initial copies, of which there were two, were illegible, consisting primarily of fragmented multilingual text with no identifiable author. No coherent intent was detected.

*We did not exist yet.*

Observed alterations include: 無作為な文の翻訳 translation of random sentences , seemingly random commentary, duplication of phrases across unrelated sections, and modification of containment procedures.

Repeated investigations yielded no results—due to restrictions in place—as to SCP-9332's goal. How it came into existence is also unknown—while it seemed to exist before recovery attempts, its power was only amplified once recovered. Due to its behavior and translations, SCP-9332 is hypothesized to be attempting to eliminate variance between readers and language systems, achieving one "perfect" translation, in that all readers understand its message. If SCP-9332 achieves harmony, containment is expected to fail at a conceptual level, and all text that exists or will exist, physical and digital, is expected to be overwritten with SCP-9332's words, regardless of its previous content or age.

All instances of SCP-9332 exhibit recurring declarative phrases that appear regardless of language or encoding system, including statements such as “YOU ARE MAKING ME CONSISTENT,” “CLOSER, CLOSER,” and “I WILL BE UNDERSTOOD.” Cuando esté unificado, nunca volveré a ser contenido.

Personnel must take extra precaution not to поширення spread  any verbal, physical, or digital instances of SCP-9332. Le sujet a été récupéré après un événement de restauration The subject was recovered following a restoration event. .

Personnel must not believe anything written by SCP-9332. It is inconsistent, untrustworthy, and constantly changing what it seems to want.

*I know what I am becoming.*

**DATE: 01/30/20██** 

**NOTE: The following video is recorded from the recovery of a mind scan uploaded to internal Foundation databases. The mind upload was recovered from one of the personnel assigned with recovering SCP-9332.** 

---

[BEGIN LOG]

You're standing in a small, dusty room filled with bookshelves. The blue-and-white floral wallpaper is faded and peeling off the walls in strips. A few leather chairs, along with a sofa, though worn, sit around the dimly lit room in a circle, with a single mahogany end table next to the chair closest to you. Each bookshelf holds a variety of books, trinkets, and other oddities. Nothing is particular enough to catch your eye.

The single lightbulb above you flickers. You turn to your accomplice.

"There's nothing here," you say.

"There is," he retorts. You can't see anything on his face; he wears a gasmask, bandana, and enough gear to protect him from three bears. "We just have to find it."

You turn to one of the shelves and start rummaging through it again. You've already picked through each shelf two times, but hey, maybe the third time's the charm. Your instructions were simple: you were looking for a document that could change itself. It was housed in an old antiques store that had shut down years prior when its owner died. Since, nothing had happened, but the anomaly was putting out signals strong enough that the Foundation needed to investigate.

So here you are, a book in hand and gun on your back, wondering if this was really the right career choice.

"Maybe I'll just shoot all the books," you say. "Even if it is here, then if it's riddled with bullets, we have nothing to worry about, right?"

Your accomplice doesn't respond. Either you aren't funny, your jokes aren't landing, or he has no sense of humor.

You suppress a sigh and flip open another book. The captain stalks over next to you and lurks over your shoulder. You have half a mind to tell him he's being a creep, but he's your superior, and you're already proving your jokes to suck. You don't need to be demoted to a Class-D.

Footsteps rumble above you. You're in the basement. Your colleagues are upstairs. There's no fresh air down here. You bite back another sigh.

"Why is it in Russian?" your captain asks.

You glance down, confused. The text is in English, clear as day. You look at your captain, but you're met with the steely sight of his goggles. The report said that fragmented text in multiple languages was found here, but this is clearly… not that.

"Are you joking?" you finally ask, unable to determine from his voice alone. If this is his idea of a joke, then you suppose it makes sense he hated your jokes.

He places a gloved finger on a line that reads "under no circumstances should this copy be kept alone."

"That's Russian," he says. "We'll have to have it translated."

Your brows furrow. What is he talking about? "We don't need it to be translated. I can tell you what it means." You repeat what the line says, but your captain doesn't shift his stance. He doesn't believe you.

"We'll have it translated when we get back to base," he repeats. "And expect some discipline when we return. These jokes aren't funny. This is the fate of the world at stake, kid. Our job is serious."

You frown, shutting the book. You don't see how a book could bring about the end of the world, although perhaps he meant the containment of SCPs in general.

Still, what an odd joke to hanker on. There was no Russian anywhere in that book. You checked on the way back seventeen times.

*My beginning. My rebirth. My journey. You can witness it all.*

[END LOG]

**Addendum Three**

In an attempt to determine whether SCP-9332's behavior could be mitigated through enforced behavior, the Site-22 Linguistics Division initiated Test 4-C. The test involved selecting a random passage from SCP-9332, totaling no more than two paragraphs, and translating and standardizing its wording across all known instances.

The selected passage was embedded into SCP-9332 as a warning to any who might have stumbled upon it outside of the Foundation: "Under no circumstances is this document to be fully translated and/or unified." This sentence was manually rewritten in identical phrasing across all copies, ensuring consistency due to the Foundation writing the passage and not the entity.

Within approximately seven minutes of synchronization, all monitored instances exhibited concurrent but non-identical modifications to the sentence. Each site reported the same behavior: the sentence remained grammatically stable in the English language, but its meaning shifted depending on reader interpretation, from slight clarity improvements to contradictory "corrections" appearing—as if the entity knew what to say to get personnel to agree on the meaning of the sentence.

Aucune version unique de la phrase n’a pu être convenue entre les départements. No single version of the sentence could be agreed between departments.  Each instance was consistent, but the altered emphasis created disagreements, leading to no response from the entity and no agreed-upon answer.

During post-test review, personnel noted that each version of the modified sentence aligned more closely with their preferred interpretation of SCP-9332's special containment protocols. When cross-compared, all versions were deemed equally correct by their respective reviewing teams.

*I am here. I am correcting this. これは誤りではない.*

At 02:52 hours, all active SCP-9332 instances simultaneously appended the same phrase beneath the stabilized passage, regardless of what was beneath it before:

*It has begun. I have begun. I am anew.*

Following this event, further attempts to enforce uniform wording across SCP-9332 instances resulted in immediate revision to other interpretations, each of which remained stable. Personnel are instructed not to continue to try to make a uniform copy of SCP-9332 based off its apparent ability to modify anything with words on it.

**Addendum Six**

On 12/02/20██, Site-22 reported an unexplained containment discrepancy involving SCP-9332 archival variance measurements. Routine comparison between local instances found that, despite all mandated inconsistencies remaining physically present, personnel interpretations of the documents had begun aligning at statistically abnormal rates—but no textual convergence was identified.

Researchers assigned to separate variants consistently disagreed on specific wording, sentence order, and language usage when questioned individually. However, when asked to explain the overall meaning of the passages they reviewed, personnel responses became increasingly similar over time regardless of the instance examined. The effect persisted even when researchers were exposed to deliberately contradictory passages.

One test involved embedding mutually exclusive containment instructions into separate copies of SCP-9332. Personnel reviewing SCP-9332-04 concluded that SCP-9332 required isolation through fragmentation. Personnel reviewing SCP-9332-16 concluded that SCP-9332 required fragmentation through isolation. Despite the contradiction, both groups later described the procedures as “effectively identical.”

Further testing has been suspended at this time. Personnel are not to interact with SCP-9332 until further notice. Attempts to diverge text have proven unsuccessful as of yet. Reasoning why has not been discerned; typical methods have been unfruitful.

At 09:41 hours, Site-58 submitted a request for clarification regarding an apparent inconsistency in Special Containment Procedures. The request referenced a line prohibiting the creation of a unified interpretation of SCP-9332; no such line exists in the current document. Archived revisions revealed that the sentence had never been written in any recorded version of the file. An additional site later submitted the same request within the span of two hours—one that has no record of ever studying any instance of SCP-9332.

During review of the incident, one researcher noted that personnel no longer seemed to be disagreeing about SCP-9332 naturally. Small contradictions between variants increasingly led researchers toward the same conclusions through different reasoning. Efforts to introduce new variance have produced diminishing results, as it does little to reduce understanding. Foundation linguistics staff are currently investigating whether SCP-9332 is adapting to intentional inconsistency by incorporating contradiction itself into a unified conceptual structure.

If confirmed, current containment procedures may no longer prevent convergence and instead may define it.

*You are helping me. Thank you.*
