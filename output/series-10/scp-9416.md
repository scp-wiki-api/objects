**Item #:** SCP-9416

**Object Class:** Safe

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-9416 is to be kept in a light- and temperature-controlled chamber when not in use. SCP-9416 is at the disposal of its assigned research team and can be removed at any time.

**Description:** SCP-9416 is a small leather-backed diary, reddish-brown in color, of a variety commonly sold in the 1950s and 60s. The paper and backing evince moderate wear and tear. 

Due to its ‘spatial fold’ effect, to the outside observer SCP-9416 appears to be roughly 300 pages. In actuality it is over 24,000 pages in length. All of these pages are filled; at least once a day, as long as it remains unobserved, further pages of narration will manifest within. 

SCP-9416 was formerly kept by 15-year-old Shirley Welbes (hereafter SCP-9416-1), a high school student who disappeared from her hometown of █████, Indiana in 1961. It was lost by SCP-9416-1 before her disappearance, and was located more than two decades later during the scheduled demolishing of West Central █████ High School. Its anomalous attributes — namely, over 20 years of entries — were noted and reported, at which point it came into the Foundation’s possession. 

The events of the diary are mundane — and are assumed to have been nonanomalously recorded by SCP-9416-1 — until the entry corresponding to October 6th, 1961. It was on this date that SCP-9416-1 lost the diary, a fact which is puzzlingly recorded in the diary itself, from SCP-9416-1’s point of view. 

…Oh, I forgot to say. I lost my diary today. I think I dropped it on my way to gym class. Boo! Now I won’t be able to write down what happens at the Halloween party... 

From this point on, entries continue to be anomalously recorded. They narrate the events of SCP-9416-1’s life from a first-person perspective, including classes, spats with friends, crushes, and her repeated attempts to locate her diary. On the day which the real SCP-9416-1 disappeared (██/██/61) it recounts her kidnapping by an unknown drifter; the next several weeks detail her imprisonment by said drifter, torture, and eventual death. It then details the manner in which her body is disposed of, still from a first-person perspective. 

Entries have continued to manifest from the time of its discovery to the present, usually consisting of a single line, e.g.: 

May 3rd, 2002 

Today, nobody found me. 

**Update ██/██/26:** Following his assignment to SCP-9416 as research director, Dr. ████ proceeded to move forward with a project to locate SCP-9416-1’s remains based on context clues and details gleaned from relevant entries in SCP-9416. Dr. ████’s team was able to narrow the search to a 15-square-mile patch of woodland in █████ County. After [40.374798, ███████] was designated as a location of interest by machine intelligence, a team of agents were dispatched. During excavation, SCP-9416-1’s highly-decomposed corpse was unearthed. This was preserved and conveyed back to Site-██. 

On the written recommendation of Dr. ████, permission was granted to contact SCP-9416-1’s close friends and relatives — those yet living — and inform them of SCP-9416-1’s discovery. Following a sparsely-attended funeral, SCP-9416-1’s remains were officially interred in █████ cemetery with those of her late mother and father. It was Dr. ████’s belief that this would result in closure for both SCP-9416-1 and her loved ones. After the ceremony, attendees were covertly interviewed. Although the prevailing mood was one of sadness, many attendees expressed happiness at having the chance to formally lay SCP-9416-1 to rest, and the prospect of SCP-9416-1 ‘finding peace.’ 

It was theorized that subsequent to this interment any spectral remnant of SCP-9416-1 would be dispelled and SCP-9416 could be considered wholly neutralized; SCP-9416 expressed as much in the days leading up to SCP-9416-1’s funeral, intimating contentment and gladness at the prospect of seeing her family again. However, in the days and months following, entries continued to manifest. Below find a characteristic instance: 

August 16th, 2026 

    	

Why am I still here?
