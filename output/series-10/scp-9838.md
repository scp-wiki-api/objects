SCP-9838 - A Roof With a View.

Authors:  and 

 ⚠️ **Content warning:** This article prominently features suicide.

**Item №:** SCP-9838

**Object Class:** Contained

**Special Containment Procedures:** Roof access to the Houston General Hospital has been permanently barred with the cooperation of the city of Houston. A set of audio speakers have been installed by Special Collections Program staff to automatically generate and share information about the decline of the United States, the increasing recidivism rate, the high propensity for addicts to relapse, nihilistic literature criticizing the global future, and predictions of a imminent nuclear apocalypse directly into the anomaly. Should an expulsion event occur, local police are to provide a standard cover story to witnesses, with threats of legal action by the Texas state government to ensure silence. 

**Description:** First brought to the attention of the Special Collections Program following an expulsion event occurring at the Providence Children's Hospital in El Paso, Texas in 1990. SCP-9838 begins, conditionally, from the rooftop of the Houston General Hospital, located in Houston, Texas. Any human subject who intentionally leaps from the roof of the building with the intent to end their own life will pass through it. The interior of SCP-9838 presents as a significantly-warped version of the Houston skyline, with the subject falling besides an infinitely tall version of the hospital, and the ground appearing to remain a constant distance away. Noise from the outside of SCP-9838 can be heard within the anomaly, though all data indicates it is subject to some distortion. 

While within SCP-9838, biological degradation and death cease to occur, with any attempts by a subject to manually end their own life resulting in no damage. Subjects remain in a state of perpetual freefall until they resolve themselves to living or fixing their lives, at which point they are violently ejected from the abnormality in the air outside a random hospital within a major city in the state of Texas. Said expulsion is at terminal velocity and has resulted in death in all three confirmed prior ejections. Special Collections Program studies of missing persons reports for the city of Houston estimates that at the current time between six to twenty people are likely within SCP-9838.

**Addendum I:** Expulsion Event 1991-4

On the 18th of February, 1991, an expulsion event occurred over the Alice Memorial Hospital in Dallas. The subject (identified as Edward Turner, missing since the 25th of December, 1990) died instantly upon impact with the concrete. The most notable personal effect recovered by Dallas PD, and subsequently sent to the Special Collections Program, was a micro-cassette recorder which survived the impact. Despite only having enough storage for two hours of audio, the cassette had approximately two months of recording on it, from which a profile of SCP-9838 was derived. While the vast majority of the recording had been significantly corrupted, a few excerpts survived. Transcripts replicated below.

##### Excerpt 1:

<Wind rushes past. Distant sounds of traffic are barely audible.>

<Silence for six minutes.>

<Subject splutters, and begins to choke.>

---

##### Excerpt 2:

<The wind is a higher pitch than before. It wavers for a second, at which point of time subject begins to monologue.>

**Subject**: “I… I don’t know what’s going on.”

**Subject**: “It’s been… days? Time feels like it’s passing differently here. And I’m all alone.”

**Subject**: “I see... the building. The sky. The city. But none of it is moving, not really."

<The wind picks up for two minutes.>

**Subject**: “Hope you don’t get bored of me.”

<Silence for forty seconds.>

**Subject**: “I'm going fucking crazy, right?"

**Subject**: “I'm stuck here forever. Fuck. Need to keep talking. Stay... mentally focused."

<Silence for thirty-two minutes. Gradually, the sound of the wind increases.>

---

##### Excerpt 3:

<The wind is a lower pitch. Subject’s teeth are chattering.>

**Subject**: “It’s c-cold here. Now it is. The sun was up, earlier.” 

**Subject**: “How long did it take to set? A week? Time feels... fuzzy. Like it isn't about the time.”

**Subject**: “Yeah, I know I’m g-going crazy. Talking to the wind. Is this my punishment?”

<The wind whistles, high, for forty seconds.>

**Subject**: “It feels so surreal now. That I did it — tried to kill myself. I can’t —”

**Subject**: “I’d do it again, though. I think.”

<The wind completely drops for a few seconds.>

**Subject**: “No, I can't think right now. Later, maybe.”

<The wind continues blowing.>

---

##### Excerpt 4:

<The wind has died down. It is preternaturally calm, as is the man’s breath.>

**Subject**: “Hannah. I loved her so much. Never properly married, but I loved her, I know it.”

**Subject**: “We had a daughter, too. Emily. She was a beautiful thing.”

**Subject**: “The most beautiful girl in the world.”

<Silence for twenty minutes, punctuated by faint weeping.>

---

##### Excerpt 5:

<The wind blows at a steady rate. Subject is already in the middle of conversation.>

**Subject**: “You know, I thought about it for a long time. Killing myself. It was the right thing to do. I deserved it. I'm not like... the others.”

**Subject**: "Had a friend, once, who offed himself too. He gave up, wasted away at the end."

**Subject**: "But I never felt more alive than when I made the choice. I felt like... I was finally doing something right."

<Silence for one minute. There is a faint *thunk* in the background.>

**Subject**: “That night, I hit her. It was our third anniversary.”

**Subject**: “Both of them, God knows. I was too drunk to work it out. I don’t even remember how I got that drunk.”

**Subject**: “Six months sober at that point.”

<Subject pauses. The wind whispers gently.>

**Subject**: “In the morning they were gone.”

**Subject**: “In the evening the police called me. A car accident.”

<The wind dies completely. This lasts for roughly three minutes. At the end, subject speaks again.>

**Subject**: “You know what's fucked up? All I can think right now is that I could really use a drink.”

<The wind picks back up.>

---

##### Excerpt 6:

<Fifteen minutes of continuous weeping. Recording ends abruptly.>

---

##### Excerpt 7:

<The wind gently whispers; subject's vocalizations are barely audible over it.>

**Subject**: “I’m so sorry…”

**Subject**: “I thought so many times, if only I could go back. Redo it all. But I can’t, I can’t.”

**Subject**: “And I would. I still love her so much. Love both of them.”

**Subject**: “It was a mistake. It was just a mistake.”

<The wind grows louder over the course of two minutes.>

**Subject**: “I swear it was a mistake.”

---

##### Excerpt 8:

<Subject is sniffling. The wind is calm again.>

**Subject**: “I met her at a restaurant, of all things. I was a temporary waiter. She was a line chef.”

**Subject**: “God knows why she agreed to be with me. I certainly didn’t deserve it.”

**Subject**: “Did you know, she could dice up a carrot in five seconds?”

**Subject**: “She did that for me, once, and, I don't know why. It was so... endearing."

**Subject**: “I just feel like... someone ought to know that.”

<Wind remains calm. Subject begins humming an unidentified tune, occasionally pausing to gasp.>

---

##### Excerpt 9:

<Subject is speaking, but audio is heavily disrupted, except for the following two lines.>

**Subject**: “This recorder was hers.”

**Subject**: “A gift.”

---

##### Excerpt 10:

<Subject is shouting over the wind, which is much louder than before. Subject occasionally pauses, as if struggling to breathe.>

**Subject**: “Recently, I’ve been thinking — well, not much else to do here — but I’ve been wondering what comes after death.”

**Subject**: “Because this isn't it, I'm certain.”

**Subject**: “Do you just... stay? Forever? That sounds kind of terrifying.”

**Subject**: “Never really got the time to think about my life, like that. Until now.”

<The wind turns to a light whistle. Subject is silent for a minute. Subject resumes vocalizations without shouting.>

**Subject**: “Nobody else here, either.”

**Subject**: “Too used to people telling me what to do, what to say, where to go. I…”

<Subject pauses for a minute.>

**Subject**: “…I would like to go to the Grand Canyon. Always wanted to as a child. Never got the chance. It would be a nice place to visit.”

---

##### Excerpt 11:

<Distortion, before a sudden clarity in audio. Subject is crying and mumbling again.>

**Subject**: “I’m sorry, Emily. I’m sorry, Hannah.”

<Subject cries for twelve minutes, gradually petering out. The wind picks up.>

**Subject**: “You can’t ever know how sorry I am.”

<Subject continues speaking, but it is inaudible due to the wind.>

---

##### Excerpt 12:

<The wind is louder than it has ever been, but subject is holding the recorder right to his face. Subject mumbles, as if speaking to himself.>

**Subject**: “This recorder had one audio clip on it, before.”

**Subject**: “From my birthday. Same day I proposed to her. She knew before, somehow, and she got it all recorded.”

**Subject**: “I tried to play it back just now.”

<A pause. The wind is screaming. Somehow, Subject's whisper is just audible.>

**Subject**: “It's not working.”

---

##### Excerpt 13:

<The wind is completely dead. Subject vocalizes slowly and deliberately.>

**Subject**: “I need to see her parents. It was my fault. I need to apologize properly. Find my own parents. They deserve better than me just going dark.”

<Subject pauses.>

**Subject**: “I need to do something.”

**Subject**: “I *want* to do something.”

<The wind begins blowing again, gently.>

---

##### Excerpt 14:

<The wind has reached the highest volume recorded thus far. Subject is shouting.>

**Subject**: “I’m ready, I think. I can see it coming. I’ve been slowing down for the last few hours. This is coming to a close.”

**Subject**: “And — and — ”

**Subject**: “I have so much I want to do. I need to apologize. I need to make amends. I need to visit the Grand Canyon. Get sober again, first. I don’t deserve it, but I want to do it. Do it all.”

**Subject**: “I can see it.”

**Subject**: “It’s slowing down, my God, and the wind is picking up and, oh God, I can see the end—“

<A crash. Recording ends abruptly.>

Following implementation of the Special Containment Procedures, no further expulsion events have been observed.
