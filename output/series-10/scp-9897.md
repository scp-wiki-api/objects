**Item #:** SCP-9897

**Object Class:** Safe

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-9897 is to be wrapped in an airtight plastic film packed between two (2) specially-molded pieces of expanded polystyrene inside an ideally 25x25x25 cm low carbon steel safe unless given authority from the HMCL Supervisor to remove the object for research purposes. The object and/or its protective case is never to be raised to a height greater than 10 cm above ground level at Site-1359 (approximately 154.30 m above sea level) unless firmly secured to mechanical equipment with sufficient robustness to ensure safe handling of the object. Impact research or residue sampling of SCP-9897 may be authorized by the HMCL Supervisor if the proposed research is approved as safe for the object. All residue samples of SCP-9897 must be stored in plastic vials and returned to the collection at Site-1359 after any potential research. Any material that has contacted SCP-9897 is considered potentially anomalous waste and must be stored on-site. Bodily contact with SCP-9897 is strictly prohibited.

**Description:** SCP-9897 is a Magtech brand .38 Super Auto full metal jacket bullet manufactured by CBC Global Ammunition approximately 8.86-8.94 mm in diameter and 15.86 mm in length with a mass of 8.31 grams. Its surface is visibly worn from a past impact, with 0.10-0.27 mm deviation in dimensions as compared to an undamaged product of the same model. Test samples taken from the object show mechanical, chemical, thermal, and optical properties identical to the brass and lead alloys (respectively) used in regular projectiles of the model. Outside of impact damage, there are no visible differences between SCP-9897 and a regular projectile of its model. The anomalous properties of SCP-9897 manifest in its kinetic behavior, with Izod impact test comparisons suggesting a kinetic energy usually equal to 1/2 mv^^█^^C,,v,,^^-2^^ with C,,v,, being a constant of approximately 0.███ m/s, causing the object to exhibit unusually low energy at speed below C,,v,, and rapidly increasing energy above the point, proportional to the █th power of the speed.

Moving SCP-9897 has required work as high as █████x more than the expected value at a speed of 80 m/s and ████x more than the expected value at a height of 20 m, having an effect similar to a seemingly increasing weight and/or mass when in motion, despite weight measurements and mass-based thermodynamic tests of the object showing consistent results regardless of speed or height. It exhibits no unusual aerodynamic properties outside of lift and drag having an exemplified effect on subcritical speed/elevation and a decreased effect on supercritical speed/elevationSubcritical speed: Speed less than C,,v,, | Supercritial speed: Speed greater than C,,v,, | Subcritical height: Height above ground level (see Addendum 2) at which maximum speed of SCP-9897 under free fall is less than C,,v,,, or a height of under ~█ mm | Supercritical height: Height above ground level at which maximum speed of SCP-9897 under free fall is greater than C,,v,,, or a height of over ~█ mm. It is as of yet unknown how movement of particles within the object is affected or how the object responds to relativistic effects, though it has been shown to clearly violate the principle of relativity (see Addendum 2 & 3). Potential adverse side effects from ingestion of SCP-9897 particulate material can include exhaustion and tachycardia at high altitudes, or, in certain tests, when a subject at a given latitude is transported nearer to the equator, thus increasing the subject’s distance from the earth’s core.

39 ammunition originally purchased alongside SCP-9897 are stored at site 1359, none of which show any anomalous properties.

**Addendum:** SCP-9897 was bought off of ██████████ in Henrietta, Texas, on 19/02/████ following the man actioning a lawsuit on CBC Global Ammunition for injury caused by faulty production in their ammunition. █████ claimed that one of CBC’s Magtech .38 Super ammunition fired on an unexpected trajectory and struck the ground in front of him, resulting in an injury to his right eye from a disturbed rock. He believed a production error to be involved, claiming the bullet exhibited unusually high weight when found, though proper weight measurements proved consistent with other ammunition of the same model. He lost the lawsuit with no major faults found in the bullet outside of probable impact damage. Still with hope of getting a compensation from CBC, █████ was originally reluctant to sell the bullet and its accompanying ammunition to a “misproduction enthusiast collector”, but eventually agreed to the offer at a price of $330.

█████ had boarded American Eagle Airlines flight ████ on 04/02/████ carrying luggage including 1.4 lbs (635 g) of handgun ammunition and a Dan Wesson M1911 pistol within checked bags. The plane began experiencing heavy stalling and issued an emergency landing 5 minutes after takeoff at an altitude of 8000 ft (2438 m) due to continued dangerous stalling and engines not operating properly. No damages to the aircraft were found upon landing.

█████ has claimed the ammunition carried on flight ████ included the “faulty bullet”. On 02/03/████ SCP-9897 was flown on a Saab 340B along with thirty four (34) 75 kg weights in each of the passenger seats to emulate the conditions of flight ████. Engines were required to work up to 7% harder to reach cruising speed than in a plane with only the weights. At an altitude of 1000 m the plane’s apparent lift generated was around 13% lower than in a plane with only the weights, which increased to 27% at 2000 m and 40% at 3000 m, at which point the test could no longer continue due to safety concerns.

**Addendum:** SCP-9897 shows normal gravitational acceleration under free fall, though impact energy usually following 1/2 mv^^█^^C,,v,,^^-2^^, thus its gravitational potential energy does not follow U = mgh, as raising the object a given distance requires more work at greater height. This as such depends on a reference height (h,,r,,), which numerous tests suggested was approximately at ground level at the respective test sites despite no apparent correlation between their elevations relative to sea level, the earth’s core, or earth’s gravity anomalies.

On 28/03/████ SCP-9897 was shipped 12 kilometers off the San Diego coast and was descended to a depth of 100 meters below sea level on a wire cable. Prior to descension, raising SCP-9897’s protective safe from 5.2 to 6.2 m above sea level via an electric pulley required approximately 62.3 J of work with presence of SCP-9897 and 53.3 J of work with absence of SCP-9897, thus requiring around 8.6 J to raise SCP-9897 itself when accounting for the ~96% efficiency of the motor used. Following ascension, raising the object from 5.2 to 6.2 m above sea level required approximately 1800 J. The test was repeated once every hour since ascension, with the first 4 tests showing 1800 J, though the 5th and later tests showing 8.6 J.

Subsequent testing has shown this apparent shift in h,,r,, to occur after around 1-14 hours of increased distance to the earth’s core. The consequences of this were explored as it would seem to be able to violate conservation of energy. In the first test conducted, the object was raised 1 m above ground level on the end of an Izod pendulum at site 1359, requiring an estimated 8.6 J of work. The pendulum was locked into place and hourly tests were conducted where the object would be raised an additional 1 m, with the first 8 showing 25.8 J and the 9th showing 8.6 J, thus having raised the object a total of 2 m with only 17.2 J of work. The pendulum was then released to impact a high-density polyethylene specimen of ASTM D256 standard dimensions, achieving a post-fracture height of 1.35-1.38 m. The test was repeated using a specimen with a thickness of 12.7 mm, resulting in a post-fracture height of 1.24-1.28 m. Using no specimen resulted in a height of 1.40 m. When the object was simply raised 2 m, requiring 35.4 J of work (including the weight of the pendulum), it achieved a post-fracture height of 1.94-1.95 m using an ASTM D256 standard specimen, 1.87-1.88 m using a 12.7 mm thick specimen, and 2.00 m using no specimen. Attempts to use this test to rotate a turbine produced no net energy. Outside of these circumstances, 1/2 mv^^█^^C,,v,,^^-2^^ has shown consistent results.

**Addendum:** High-speed video recording of tests where SCP-9897 was fired from an unsecured pneumatic gun suggest expected recoil and momentum conservation considering the anomalous projectile speed. Similar tests onboard moving railcars suggest the object does not experience the Oberth effect, which had been explored as another potential mechanism for which the object could violate conservation of energy. Tests where the object was put in sustained motion on a moving train for up to 20 hours failed to provide any evidence for a periodic shift in v,,r,, alike that in the object’s h,,r,,.
